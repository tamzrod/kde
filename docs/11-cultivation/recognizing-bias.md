# Recognizing Bias

---

## The Simple Idea

You think you're objective. You're probably not.

Everyone has biases. The question isn't whether you have them—you do. The question is whether you can recognize them.

This skill teaches you to identify cognitive biases that distort reasoning and take steps to mitigate them.

---

## Real-World Observation

Two investigators examine the same evidence about a system failure:

1. Investigator A thinks the vendor is unreliable
2. Investigator B has no prior opinion

Both examine evidence that the vendor's component failed. Investigator A concludes the vendor is at fault. Investigator B concludes the component failed but doesn't assign blame.

Same evidence. Different conclusions. Bias is invisible to the biased.

## Common Cognitive Biases

### Confirmation Bias

**What it is**: Seeking evidence that confirms your beliefs

| Instead of... | Try... |
|--------------|--------|
| "Let me find evidence I'm right" | "Let me find evidence I'm wrong" |
| Ignoring contradictory data | Actively seeking contradictory data |
| Interpreting ambiguity favorably | Interpreting ambiguity neutrally |

**How to counter**: Ask "What evidence would disprove my hypothesis?"

### Anchoring Bias

**What it is**: Relying too heavily on the first piece of information

| Instead of... | Try... |
|--------------|--------|
| "My first guess is probably right" | "Let me consider multiple starting points" |
| Sticking with initial hypothesis | Revisiting initial assumptions |

**How to counter**: Consider alternative explanations before settling on one.

### Availability Bias

**What it is**: Overestimating the importance of easily recalled information

| Instead of... | Try... |
|--------------|--------|
| "I remember X, so X is important" | "How representative is this?" |
| Focusing on recent events | Considering historical patterns |

**How to counter**: Seek systematic data, not just memorable examples.

### Sunk Cost Fallacy

**What it is**: Continuing because of past investment, not merit

| Instead of... | Try... |
|--------------|--------|
| "We've already spent so much time" | "What would I decide if starting fresh?" |
| Sticking with a bad investigation | Knowing when to stop |

**How to counter**: Evaluate each decision on its own merits.

### Authority Bias

**What it is**: Accepting expert opinions without question

| Instead of... | Try... |
|--------------|--------|
| "An expert said it, so it's true" | "What evidence supports this?" |
| Not questioning experts | Respectfully requesting evidence |

**How to counter**: Experts can be wrong. Ask for evidence.

### Groupthink

**What it is**: Conforming to group consensus without critical evaluation

| Instead of... | Try... |
|--------------|--------|
| "Everyone agrees, so it must be right" | "What are the dissenting views?" |
| Avoiding conflict | Encouraging dissent |

**How to counter**: Assign someone to play devil's advocate.

---

## Bias Detection Checklist

Ask yourself:

- [ ] Am I seeking evidence that confirms my beliefs?
- [ ] Am I dismissing evidence that contradicts my beliefs?
- [ ] Am I relying too heavily on first impressions?
- [ ] Am I favoring easily recalled information?
- [ ] Am I continuing because of past investment?
- [ ] Am I accepting expert opinions without question?
- [ ] Am I conforming to group consensus?

## Bias Mitigation Techniques

### For Confirmation Bias

```markdown
## Hypothesis: Temperature causes failures

### Evidence FOR hypothesis
- High temperature preceded failure
- System designed to operate below 100°C

### Evidence AGAINST hypothesis
- Similar failures occurred at normal temperature
- Cooling system showed no fault

### Conclusion
Evidence is mixed. Cannot confirm temperature as sole cause.
```

### For Anchoring Bias

```markdown
## Initial Hypothesis
"Vendor's component is unreliable."

## Alternative Explanations
1. Installation error
2. Environmental factors
3. Configuration issue
4. Interaction with other components

## Evaluation
Multiple explanations possible. Need more evidence before conclusion.
```

### For Sunk Cost Fallacy

```markdown
## Time Invested
Investigation has consumed 3 days.

## Current State
No clear evidence of root cause.

## Fresh Evaluation
If starting today with this evidence, would I continue?

Recommendation: Document current findings and conclude.
```

## Recognizing Bias in Others

Bias isn't just personal. It can be systemic:

| Source | Bias Type | Indicator |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Reports | Selection bias | Only shows successful cases |
| Studies | Funding bias | Sponsor benefits from outcome |
| Documentation | Survivorship bias | Only covers systems that didn't fail |
| Experts | Overconfidence | High certainty without evidence |

## Practice Exercise

**Situation**: You believe the new team member is the cause of recent failures. You notice mistakes they make but don't notice their successes.

**Identify the bias**: Confirmation bias

**What to do**:
1. Acknowledge the bias: "I'm looking for evidence I'm right"
2. Seek contrary evidence: "What have they done well?"
3. Evaluate objectively: "What's the actual evidence?"

**Write your assessment**:

```markdown
## Bias Check

### Observed Behavior
Noticed 3 mistakes by new team member this week.

### Unnoticed Behavior
Didn't notice 5 successful tasks completed.

### Bias Assessment
CONFIRMATION BIAS detected. Seeking evidence that confirms negative assessment.

### Correction
Gathered data shows: 3 mistakes, 5 successes.
Performance is within normal range.
```

## Summary

| Bias | What It Is | Counter |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Confirmation** | Seeking confirming evidence | Seek disconfirming evidence |
| **Anchoring** | Over-relying on first info | Consider alternatives |
| **Availability** | Overweighting memorable info | Seek systematic data |
| **Sunk Cost** | Continuing due to past investment | Evaluate on merits |
| **Authority** | Accepting experts uncritically | Request evidence |
| **Groupthink** | Conforming to consensus | Encourage dissent |

Bias is invisible to the biased. The first step is recognizing you have biases.
