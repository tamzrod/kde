# Handling Uncertainty

---

## The Simple Idea

Certainty is rare. Most real questions have incomplete answers. Good investigators work with uncertainty rather than pretending it doesn't exist.

This skill teaches you to express uncertainty appropriately and make decisions with incomplete information.

---

## Real-World Observation

A doctor orders a test. The result is positive. But the test is 95% accurate. The patient asks: "Do I have the disease?"

The honest answer: "The test is positive. There's about a 95% chance you have it. But we won't know for certain until we do more tests."

The doctor could have said "You have it" or "You don't have it." Instead, they expressed the uncertainty honestly.

This is handling uncertainty. It means communicating what you know, what you don't know, and how confident you are.

---

## Degrees of Confidence

Express confidence in levels:

| Level | Meaning | Evidence Required |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| **Certain** | No reasonable doubt | Overwhelming evidence, no competing explanations |
| **High confidence** | Very likely | Strong evidence, minor gaps |
| **Moderate confidence** | Likely | Good evidence, some gaps |
| **Low confidence** | Possible but uncertain | Weak evidence, significant gaps |
| **Speculation** | Guessing | No evidence, intuition only |

---

## Expressing Uncertainty

### The Uncertainty Spectrum

```
CERTAIN ────────────────────────────────────────── UNCERTAIN
  │         │           │           │         │        │
KNOWING   HIGH       MODERATE     LOW     SPECULATION
```

### How to Express Uncertainty

| Instead of... | Say... |
|--------------|--------|
| "X is true" | "Evidence suggests X is true" |
| "X caused Y" | "X is associated with Y" |
| "We should do X" | "X is the most likely option given current evidence" |
| "X is the answer" | "X best explains the available evidence" |

## Working with Insufficient Evidence

When evidence is incomplete:

### Step 1: Acknowledge What You Don't Know

```markdown
## Uncertainty Assessment

### Known
- Temperature exceeded 100°C for 3 hours
- System failed during that period

### Unknown
- Whether temperature caused the failure
- Whether other factors contributed
- Whether this has happened before

### Confidence
LOW to MODERATE - correlation established, causation not proven.
```

### Step 2: Make Decisions Anyway

Incomplete evidence doesn't mean no decisions. It means:

- Weight decisions by confidence
- Monitor for disconfirming evidence
- Be ready to change course

### Step 3: Communicate Uncertainty

Be explicit about what you know and don't know:

```markdown
## Conclusion

Based on current evidence, temperature exceeding 100°C is the most likely 
cause of system failure (MODERATE confidence).

However:
- Correlation does not prove causation
- Other factors may contribute
- Further investigation needed

Recommendation: Address temperature management as priority, but monitor for 
alternative causes.
```

## When Uncertainty Is Too High

Sometimes uncertainty is so high that no conclusion is warranted.

### Indicators of Excessive Uncertainty

| Indicator | What It Means |
|-----------|---------------|
| Conflicting evidence | Multiple strong sources disagree |
| No primary sources | Only secondary or tertiary evidence |
| High novelty | No prior research in this area |
| Complex causality | Many potential causes |
| Weak methodology | Evidence gathering method unreliable |

### When to Say "I Don't Know"

Say "I don't know" when:
- Evidence is insufficient to support any conclusion
- Conflicting evidence is equally strong
- The question cannot be answered with available evidence

This isn't failure. It's intellectual honesty.

---

## Communicating Uncertainty Checklist

Before concluding, check:

- [ ] Have I stated my confidence level?
- [ ] Have I distinguished correlation from causation?
- [ ] Have I identified what I don't know?
- [ ] Have I proposed next steps?
- [ ] Have I avoided false certainty?
- [ ] Have I avoided false humility?

## Practice Exercise

**Situation**: You investigated why a system failed. You found:
- High temperature preceded failure
- But the cooling system also had a known bug
- Historical data shows similar failures at low temperatures

**Express your uncertainty:**

```markdown
## Conclusion with Uncertainty

### Findings
1. High temperature preceded this failure
2. Cooling system has known bug
3. Historical failures occur at low temperature too

### Confidence Assessment
- Temperature: Associated but causation unclear
- Cooling bug: May contribute but didn't prevent other failures
- Historical pattern: Suggests multiple failure modes

### Uncertainty Statement
MODERATE uncertainty. Evidence suggests multiple potential causes. 
Temperature may be one factor but is not the sole cause.

Cannot confidently identify a single cause.
```

---

## Summary

| Concept | Key Takeaway |
|---------|-------------|
| **Confidence levels** | Certain, high, moderate, low, speculation |
| **Expressing uncertainty** | "Evidence suggests" not "X is true" |
| **Working with uncertainty** | Make decisions weighted by confidence |
| **Knowing limits** | Sometimes uncertainty is too high for conclusions |

The goal isn't to eliminate uncertainty. It's to work with it honestly.
