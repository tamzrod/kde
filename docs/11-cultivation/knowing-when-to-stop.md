# Knowing When to Stop

---

## The Simple Idea

Investigation can continue forever. There's always more evidence to gather, more sources to check, more questions to ask.

Knowing when to stop is a skill. It's about recognizing when you have enough evidence to act.

This skill teaches you when to conclude and how to document what remains uninvestigated.

---

## Real-World Observation

A journalist is investigating a company. They've gathered:
- 10 interviews
- 500 documents
- 3 years of financial records

They have enough evidence to write the story. But they keep finding new angles:
- "What if there's more in the overseas accounts?"
- "I should interview one more person."
- "Maybe I missed something in the documents."

They could investigate forever. Eventually, they need to write the story with what they have.

This is knowing when to stop. It's about recognizing sufficiency.

---

## The Diminishing Returns Problem

Evidence gathering follows diminishing returns:

| Evidence Gathered | New Insights | Time per Insight |
|-------------------|---------------|------------------|
| 10% of evidence | Major insights | Low |
| 30% of evidence | Significant insights | Medium |
| 60% of evidence | Moderate insights | High |
| 80% of evidence | Minor insights | Very high |
| 100% of evidence | Marginal insights | Extreme |

At some point, additional evidence costs more than it's worth.

## Sufficiency Criteria

### When You Have Enough Evidence

| Criterion | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| **Primary question answered** | Can you answer the main question? |
| **Confidence threshold met** | Is confidence high enough to act? |
| **Major alternatives addressed** | Have you addressed the main alternatives? |
| **Evidence is representative** | Is your evidence typical, not exceptional? |
| **Time budget exhausted** | Have you used your allocated time? |

### When You Need More Evidence

| Criterion | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| **Primary question unanswered** | Can you answer the main question? |
| **Confidence too low** | Is confidence too low to act? |
| **Major alternatives unexplored** | Are there unexplored alternatives? |
| **Evidence is atypical** | Did you only find exceptional cases? |
| **Decision is high-stakes** | Will this decision have significant consequences? |

## The Iterate vs. Conclude Decision

### The Decision Matrix

| Situation | Decision |
|-----------|----------|
| Evidence sufficient + low stakes | Conclude |
| Evidence sufficient + high stakes | Conclude with caution |
| Evidence insufficient + low stakes | Conclude with low confidence |
| Evidence insufficient + high stakes | Investigate more |

### Making the Decision

```markdown
## Conclude vs. Investigate More

### Evidence Status
- Primary question: Answered (partial)
- Confidence: MODERATE
- Alternatives: Addressed 3 of 5
- Time used: 3 of 5 days

### Decision Factors
- Stakes: MEDIUM (moderate business impact)
- Evidence: SUFFICIENT (enough to act, but gaps remain)
- Time: LIMITED (2 days remain in budget)

### Decision
CONCLUDE with documented gaps.

### Confidence
MODERATE. Actionable but with noted limitations.
```

## Documenting Remaining Investigation

When you conclude, document what you didn't investigate:

```markdown
## What Remains Uninvestigated

### Out of Scope
- Overseas operations (different investigation)
- Historical data before 2024 (data unavailable)
- Competitor comparison (out of scope)

### Future Investigation
- Customer impact survey (planned for Q4)
- Vendor reliability assessment (scheduled)
- Process improvement validation (deferred)

### Known Gaps
- Evidence from third parties (unavailable)
- Statistical significance of findings (sample size)
- Alternative explanations (less likely but possible)
```

## Warning Signs of Never Stopping

Recognize these patterns:

| Pattern | What It Means |
|---------|---------------|
| "Just one more source" | Scope creep |
| "I need to be certain" | Perfectionism |
| "What if I missed something?" | Anxiety-driven investigation |
| "I should check everything" | Over-comprehensiveness |
| "The answer isn't clear yet" | Waiting for certainty |

## Stopping Strategies

### Time-Boxing

Set a fixed time budget:
- 1 day for quick questions
- 1 week for moderate questions
- 1 month for complex questions

Stop when time is exhausted.

### Decision-Boxing

Set a fixed decision threshold:
- "I'll investigate until confidence reaches MODERATE"
- "I'll investigate until I find evidence for or against"
- "I'll investigate until I've addressed top 3 alternatives"

Stop when threshold is met.

### Evidence-Boxing

Set a fixed evidence budget:
- "I'll interview 10 sources maximum"
- "I'll review 100 documents maximum"
- "I'll search 5 databases maximum"

Stop when evidence budget is exhausted.

## Practice Exercise

**Situation**: You've investigated a system failure for 5 days. You have:
- Root cause identified (temperature exceeding threshold)
- 3 alternative causes ruled out
- 2 minor causes identified but not fully investigated
- Confidence level: MODERATE

**Should you stop?**

**Analysis**:

```markdown
## Stop or Continue Analysis

### Evidence Gathered
- Primary question: ROOT CAUSE IDENTIFIED
- Confidence: MODERATE
- Alternatives: 3 of 5 addressed
- Time: 5 of 5 days budgeted

### Decision Factors
- Stakes: MEDIUM (actionable recommendation possible)
- Evidence: SUFFICIENT (can recommend temperature management)
- Time: EXHAUSTED (at budget)

### Remaining Work
- 2 minor causes partially investigated
- Would require additional time and resources

### Decision
CONCLUDE.

### Rationale
- Primary question answered
- Confidence sufficient for action
- Time budget exhausted
- Minor causes don't change primary recommendation

### Documentation
Document remaining work for future investigation.
```

## Summary

| Concept | Key Takeaway |
|---------|-------------|
| **Diminishing returns** | Additional evidence costs more than it's worth |
| **Sufficiency** | Having enough evidence to act |
| **Iterate vs. conclude** | Depends on evidence and stakes |
| **Documentation** | Record what remains uninvestigated |

Perfect investigation is impossible. Good investigation knows when to stop.
