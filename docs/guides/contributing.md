# Contributing Guide

**How to participate in KDE research**

---

## Who Can Contribute?

**Anyone.** KDE welcomes:
- Researchers seeking validated knowledge
- Developers integrating KDE methods
- Domain experts sharing knowledge
- Reviewers validating findings
- Questioners asking the right questions

---

## Ways to Contribute

| Way | What You Do | Your Role |
|-----|-------------|-----------|
| **Ask Questions** | Define what needs research | Questioner |
| **Conduct Research** | Investigate and gather evidence | Investigator |
| **Review Work** | Check evidence and logic | Reviewer |
| **Approve Knowledge** | Make final decisions | Approver |
| **Improve Documentation** | Make KDE clearer | Contributor |

## Step-by-Step Contribution Guide

### Step 1: Choose Your Role

| If you want to... | Your role is... |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| Ask "what should we research?" | Questioner |
| Do the actual research | Investigator |
| Check if research is correct | Reviewer |
| Make final approval decisions | Approver |

### Step 2: Understand the Rules

Before contributing, understand:

1. **Evidence is required** - Every claim needs sources
2. **Humans approve** - AI cannot approve its own work
3. **Transparency** - The process is visible to all

### Step 3: Submit Your Contribution

**For Questioners:**
```
Format: "What is [topic]?"
Example: "What are the best practices for API authentication?"
```

**For Investigators:**
```
Format: Investigation with evidence
Required: Sources for every claim
Output: Draft document with citations
```

**For Reviewers:**
```
Format: Review checklist
Check: Evidence quality
Check: Logic and reasoning
Check: Completeness
```

### Step 4: Await Process

After submission:
1. Your contribution enters the workflow
2. It may be reviewed
3. You may receive feedback
4. Once approved, it becomes knowledge

## Contribution Types

### 1. Asking Questions

**Best for**: Anyone with a knowledge gap

**How**:
1. Identify what you need to know
2. Submit as a question
3. Await investigation

**Tips**:
- Be specific
- Provide context
- Explain why it matters

### 2. Conducting Research

**Best for**: Subject matter experts

**How**:
1. Accept an investigation
2. Gather evidence
3. Document findings
4. Submit for review

**Requirements**:
- Every claim needs a source
- Follow the Engine methodology
- Be transparent about limitations

### 3. Reviewing Work

**Best for**: Critical thinkers

**How**:
1. Receive findings to review
2. Check evidence quality
3. Verify reasoning
4. Provide feedback

**Review Checklist**:
- [ ] Are sources credible?
- [ ] Is evidence sufficient?
- [ ] Are conclusions justified?
- [ ] Is anything missing?

### 4. Approving Knowledge

**Best for**: Authority figures

**Authority Required**: You must be designated as an Approver

**How**:
1. Review validated work
2. Make promotion decision
3. Document rationale

## Quality Standards

### What Makes Good Evidence?

| Quality | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Primary sources** | Original research, official docs |
| **Credible sources** | Peer-reviewed, established organizations |
| **Recent** | Current data (when applicable) |
| **Relevant** | Directly supports the claim |
| **Verifiable** | Others can check it |

### What Makes Good Findings?

| Quality | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Clear** | Easy to understand |
| **Complete** | Covers the scope |
| **Supported** | Evidence backs every claim |
| **Honest** | Acknowledges limitations |
| **Actionable** | Reader knows what to do |

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why It's Bad | Correction |
|---------|--------------|------------|
| Claims without sources | Undermines credibility | Add citations |
| opinions as facts | Confuses readers | Label as inference |
| Ignoring contrary evidence | Biased conclusions | Address opposing views |
| Overgeneralizing | Weak findings | Qualify claims |
| Skipping review | Quality issues | Always submit for review |
