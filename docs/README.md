# Knowledge Discovery Engine (KDE)

**Plain-Language Documentation for Humans**

---

## What is KDE?

**KDE is a scientific methodology for discovering and validating knowledge using AI agents under human oversight.**

That's it. That's the core idea.

### In Simple Terms

Imagine you have an AI assistant that helps you research topics. Without KDE:
- The AI might make things up
- You wouldn't know what's fact vs. opinion
- The AI might keep working without asking

With KDE:
- AI works **step-by-step** like a scientist
- AI **shows evidence** for every claim
- **Humans decide** when to approve work
- **Humans decide** what becomes "official" knowledge

---

## The Five Human Oversight Rules

KDE has five rules that ensure humans stay in control:

| Rule | What It Means |
|------|---------------|
| **Humans Control the Process** | AI waits for human permission before starting new work |
| **Humans Approve the Work** | AI cannot approve its own output |
| **Humans Promote Knowledge** | Only humans can make knowledge "official" |
| **Evidence is Clear** | Facts are marked as facts, opinions as opinions |
| **Change Requires Evidence** | New ideas need justification, not just assertions |

## Why KDE Exists

Traditional AI research has problems:

| Problem | KDE Solution |
|---------|--------------|
| AI makes things up | Require evidence for every claim |
| No clear approval process | Humans approve work |
| Opinions disguised as facts | Clear labels for evidence vs. inference |
| AI keeps working without check | Human authorization required for each step |

---

## Key Principles

### 1. Evidence-Based

Every claim in KDE must be backed by documented evidence. No assertions without sources.

### 2. Human-in-the-Loop

Humans make all critical decisions:
- What questions to investigate
- When to approve results
- When to promote knowledge

### 3. Transparent Process

The methodology is documented. Anyone can understand how KDE produces knowledge.

### 4. Scientific Discipline

KDE follows a structured scientific process:
1. Define the question
2. Gather evidence
3. Analyze findings
4. Draw conclusions
5. Validate with humans
