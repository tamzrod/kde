# SIDE STORY: RESIDENT EVIL — THE FAILED CLONES

I was thinking about failures.

Not my own failures. Not specific ones, anyway. Just the general question: what do you do with things that don't work?

In software, we delete them. Move on. Start again. The failed implementation gets archived or deleted, and we try something else.

I had been doing this for years. Building things that didn't work. Deleting them. Building again.

The failures accumulated. So did the questions.

---

I don't remember how many projects I had abandoned.

Repositories I no longer opened. Implementations I no longer understood. Architectures that seemed promising until they weren't.

Each one taught me something. I was sure of that. But what I had learned seemed to disappear along with the code. I could remember that a project had failed. I couldn't always remember why.

The implementation died. The lesson faded.

I kept starting over.

---

At some point, I started keeping notes.

Not about what I was building. About what I was learning.

The notes were simple. What I tried. What happened. What I would do differently.

I did not think of this as evidence collection. I thought of it as not forgetting.

But that was what I was doing: collecting evidence. Preserving what the failures revealed.

The implementation could be deleted. The evidence remained.

---

I thought about failed experiments.

Not just my own. The general pattern.

A scientist runs an experiment. The hypothesis is wrong. The experiment fails. Does the scientist discard the results?

No. The results are the evidence. The evidence says: this hypothesis is wrong. This path does not lead where we thought.

The experiment failed. The evidence succeeded. It told us something we did not know before.

I had been thinking about failures as waste. As steps backward. As evidence that I had made a mistake.

But failures are evidence. The question is whether we preserve the evidence or discard it along with the implementation.

---

I thought about the movie.

Not the plot. Not the characters. Just the premise.

Hundreds of clones. Most failed. Experimentation continued regardless.

The pattern seemed obvious, once I saw it. The clones were not the product. The clones were experiments.

Each failure generated evidence. Each failure narrowed the search. Each iteration carried forward what was learned.

The value was never in preserving every clone. The value was in preserving what every clone revealed.

I had been thinking about prototypes the wrong way.

---

The prototype is not the product.

The prototype is an experiment.

The experiment generates evidence.

The evidence tells us something.

That something becomes knowledge.

Knowledge informs the next experiment.

The implementation may be deleted. The knowledge survives.

I had been preserving implementations and losing knowledge. The opposite of what worked.

---

I don't remember when I started changing.

Maybe it was when I deleted a repository and felt nothing. Because I had already taken what it had to teach.

Maybe it was when I kept notes after a failure and found them useful later.

Maybe it was just something that accumulated over time, the way understanding does.

What I remember is the feeling: the prototype is disposable. The evidence is not.

The implementation can be rebuilt. The knowledge cannot be recreated once it is lost.

---

The failed clones were never the product.

They were experiments.

Each failure reduced uncertainty.

Each failure generated evidence.

Each iteration carried forward what was learned.

The value was never in preserving every clone.

The value was in preserving what every clone revealed.

I had been building things wrong, I realized. I had been preserving code and losing lessons. The opposite of what worked.

---

I think about this now, when I start something new.

What am I trying to learn? What evidence will this experiment generate? What will I preserve when the implementation is deleted?

The questions feel different now. The focus shifted from building to understanding. From implementing to discovering.

A prototype is disposable. Evidence is not. Implementations may be deleted. Repositories may disappear. Sandboxes may be abandoned.

But validated discoveries should always become part of the engineering lineage.

---

Engineering maturity is measured not by how many prototypes survive.

It is measured by how much knowledge survives each discarded prototype.

The failed clones were not waste.

They were evidence.

And evidence, carefully preserved, becomes the foundation for everything that comes after.
