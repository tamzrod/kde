## Investigation Summary

This investigation examines how the Law of Diminishing Returns reveals a universal engineering principle that influenced KDE's approach to knowing when an investigation has reached practical completion.

---

## The Engineering Problem

### The Question

When does an investigation become complete?

How much evidence is enough? Can certainty continue increasing forever? Or does engineering eventually reach a point where additional effort produces very little additional understanding?

### The Pattern

| Investigation Phase | Discovery Value | Effort Required |
|--------------------|----------------|----------------|
| Initial | Major discovery | Baseline |
| Second | Significant refinement | Similar |
| Third | Minor improvement | Similar |
| Later | Tiny refinement | Similar |
| Final | Repetition | Similar |

The first investigation usually produces the largest discoveries. Later investigations often produce increasingly smaller improvements.

---

## The Principle

### Diminishing Returns in Engineering

| Phase | What Happens |
|-------|-------------|
| Early | Major uncertainty removed |
| Middle | Uncertainty refined |
| Late | Uncertainty confirmed |
| Final | Mostly repetition |

### The Transition Points

- When does refinement become optimization?
- When does optimization become overengineering?
- When does verification become repetition?
