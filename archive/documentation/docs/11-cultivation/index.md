# Cultivation

---

## The Simple Idea

Reading documentation teaches you what KDE is. Cultivation teaches you how to think like an investigator.

The gap between knowing a methodology and thinking with it is the cultivation gap. This section bridges that gap.

---

## Why This Section Exists

You can read every document in this repository and still not know how to investigate. Documentation explains what KDE is. Cultivation develops how you think.

This section is for people who want to become investigators, not just people who want to understand KDE.

---

## What You'll Develop

| Skill | What It Means | Why It Matters |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| **Hypothesis Formation** | Turning questions into testable ideas | Investigations start with hypotheses |
| **Evidence Discovery** | Finding evidence when it isn't obvious | Evidence is the foundation of knowledge |
| **Source Evaluation** | Assessing evidence quality | Not all evidence is equal |
| **Uncertainty Tolerance** | Working with incomplete information | Most real questions have uncertain answers |
| **Bias Recognition** | Identifying bias in reasoning | Self-deception is the investigator's greatest risk |
| **Question Design** | Framing questions that can be answered | Bad questions produce bad answers |
| **Knowing When to Stop** | Recognizing sufficient investigation | Investigation can continue forever |
| **Investigative Habits** | Daily practices of effective investigators | Skills require practice |

---

## The Eight Skills

### 1. Developing Hypotheses

Transform vague questions into testable hypotheses. Learn what makes a hypothesis testable, how to make predictions, and how to recognize unfalsifiable claims.

**[Developing Hypotheses](developing-hypotheses.md)**

### 2. Finding Evidence

Learn systematic approaches to evidence discovery. Find evidence when you don't know where to look, recognize when evidence doesn't exist, and document absence of evidence.

**[Finding Evidence](finding-evidence.md)**

### 3. Evaluating Sources

Assess evidence quality with critical thinking. Distinguish primary from secondary sources, recognize bias, and handle conflicting evidence.

**[Evaluating Sources](evaluating-sources.md)**

### 4. Handling Uncertainty

Work productively with incomplete information. Express confidence appropriately, recognize when uncertainty is too high, and communicate what you don't know.

**[Handling Uncertainty](handling-uncertainty.md)**

### 5. Recognizing Bias

Identify bias in yourself and others. Learn common cognitive biases, seek disconfirming evidence, and question your own conclusions.

**[Recognizing Bias](recognizing-bias.md)**

### 6. Formulating Questions

Frame questions that can be investigated. Scope questions appropriately, break complex questions into parts, and recognize unanswerable questions.

**[Formulating Questions](formulating-questions.md)**

### 7. Knowing When to Stop

Recognize sufficient investigation. Understand diminishing returns, document what remains uninvestigated, and make the iterate vs. conclude decision.

**[Knowing When to Stop](knowing-when-to-stop.md)**

### 8. Investigative Habits

Develop daily practices of effective investigators. Maintain questioning habits, document reasoning, and practice reflection.

**[Investigative Habits](investigative-habits.md)**

---

## How to Use This Section

### For Beginners

Start with Formulating Questions and Developing Hypotheses. These skills form the foundation for everything else.

### For Practitioners

Review Recognizing Bias and Knowing When to Stop. These skills prevent the mistakes that experienced investigators make.

### For Everyone

Work through Investigative Habits last. Skills require practice, not just reading.

## The Difference Between Knowing and Thinking

| Knowing | Thinking |
|---------|----------|
| "Evidence is required" | "What evidence exists for this claim?" |
| "Hypotheses must be testable" | "Is my hypothesis actually testable?" |
| "Bias should be recognized" | "Am I confirming my own beliefs?" |
| "Uncertainty must be communicated" | "How confident am I, and why?" |

The documents in this section will help you make these transitions.
